This is the final project for [STA303: Artificial Intelligence](https://fangkongx.github.io/Teaching/STA303/Fall2025/index.html), done with two teammates.
My part covers *PPO*, *BC* and the *World Model*.
We compared a range of RL algorithms, using CartPole-v1 as a common testbed.
A run is solved when the 100-episode average passes 475 (the max is 500).

Methods we tested and analyzed:
- Online: *DQN*, *PPO*, *A2C*, and *Rainbow*.
- Offline: *BC*, *BCQ*, each trying to replicate or improve on an expert policy.
- Special: a simplified *World Model*, just for interest.

- **DQN** looks fine in the first run, but a longer training run collapses repeatedly after reaching 500. We read this as catastrophic forgetting: the Q-function is overwritten by recent, almost-all-success data.
- **PPO** (mine) reached 500 in evaluation. Rollout length 128 beat 64 and 256, and the number of epochs per sample (EPS) was the most sensitive knob. Too few and learning crawls, too many and samples get clipped out of the trust region.
- **A2C** needs the critic to learn faster than the actor (about 10x in our runs). Otherwise the baseline lags and the policy gradient turns noisy. Entropy regularization stopped it from settling early.
- **Rainbow** fixes DQN's collapse. It combines noisy nets, distributional values, prioritized replay, n-step returns, double and dueling DQN. We ran ablations on four of them, and removing any one slowed convergence.

## Offline

BC and BCQ learn from trajectories collected by the trained PPO expert (about 100 episodes), with no environment interaction during training.

### Behavior Cloning (BC)

BC is plain supervised learning from state to action. The dataset is 50,000 state–action pairs generated by the trained PPO teacher.

It reaches 500 with a 3-layer MLP: Linear($4\times d$)$\to$ReLU$\to$Linear($d\times d$)$\to$ReLU$\to$Linear($d\times2$), converging in about 15 epochs.

An ablation shows that even $d=4$ still reaches 500. CartPole has only 4 state dimensions and 2 actions, so this is a very simple classification problem and a tiny MLP is enough.

### BCQ

BCQ only lets the Q-learning target use actions the behavior policy would plausibly take, which avoids out-of-distribution value overestimation. We used a threshold of 0.3. It is also the slowest offline method here, needing about 200 epochs.

### World model

Inspired by [Ha &amp; Schmidhuber](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122), we wondered whether a small model could learn the dynamics from randomly generated data, so that a controller can be optimized inside the world model instead of the real environment.
There are two parts.

- **The world model** $M$ predicts the next state from the current state and action, $\hat s_{t+1} = M(s_t, a_t)$. It is a MLP trained on a dataset of 1,000 episodes (about 13,500 transitions) from random interactions.
- **The controller** $C$ is also a small policy MLP. Its parameters are optimized with the cross-entropy method (CEM), entirely inside $M$.

Training the controller is cheap and never touches the real environment.

Two tricks make $M$ accurate enough to trust.

1. **Normalization.** CartPole is very sensitive to the scale of its state.
Plain MSE on raw states performed badly and produced large out-of-distribution errors,
so both the inputs and the outputs are standardized:

   $$
   \hat s = \frac{s - \mu}{\sigma}
   $$

2. **Multi-step autoregressive loss.** Predicting only one step ahead gave a model that looked fine,
since its loss stayed tiny, but drifted when rolled out.
So the model is trained to predict $H = 10$ steps autoregressively,
and the loss sums the squared error over the horizon:

   $$
   \mathcal{L} = \sum_{k=1}^{H} \left\lVert \hat s_{t+k} - s_{t+k} \right\rVert^2
   $$

The results are below. With a hidden size of 64 for $M$, the loss drops below $10^{-3}$ within about 15 epochs.
The trained controller scores 500 in the real environment, across different random seeds.
Doubling the hidden size of $M$ was slightly worse: an average of 499.73 over 100 episodes, with occasional drops to 485.
We suspect overfitting and the resulting out-of-distribution states.

## Convergence speed

Approximate points of convergence, read off the training curves. Online and offline are not directly comparable, since one counts episodes and the other epochs.

| Setting | Method | Converges at | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Online | Rainbow | ~200 episodes | |
| Online | DQN | ~300 episodes | collapses again at ~600 |
| Online | PPO | ~500 episodes | |
| Online | A2C | ~800 episodes | |
| Offline | World model (M) | ~10 epochs | dynamics model |
| Offline | BC | ~15 epochs | |
| Offline | World model (C) | ~20 epochs | CEM controller |
| Offline | BCQ | ~200 epochs | |

Rainbow was the most efficient online method here. Offline, BC is hard to beat on a task this simple, and the world model converges just as quickly.
